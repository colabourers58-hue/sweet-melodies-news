Majority and Nana B clash over passport dispute and evacuation spending

The NPP National Vice Chairman denied lobbying to keep a service passport while raising questions about Foreign Ministry expenditure.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

The Majority Caucus in Parliament and New Patriotic Party executive Henry Nana Boakye traded accusations on Tuesday in Accra over service passport requests and foreign evacuation spending.

Why it matters

The disagreement in Parliament raises questions about transparency surrounding public funds spent on evacuating citizens abroad. It also affects how Ghanaians view accountability between government officials and opposition lawmakers.

The details

According to MyJoyOnline and 3News, the caucus held a media briefing in the Greater Accra Region, where Salaga South Member of Parliament Hajia Zuwera Mohammed Ibrahimah alleged that Deputy Ranking Member Nana Asafo-Adjaye and Mr Boakye urged Foreign Affairs Minister Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa to let Mr Boakye keep his service passport.

Yen.com.gh reported that the Majority defended the minister’s refusal, citing a presidential directive requiring former officeholders to surrender diplomatic and service passports. Mrs Ibrahimah stated that “the minister’s strict compliance with the Presidential Directive, and refusal to be corrupted, must clearly be the reason for the consistent baseless unwarranted personal attacks”.

Adom News reported that the caucus warned against using parliamentary oversight to settle personal scores. Mrs Ibrahimah noted that public officials must operate within legal limits, adding that “he that comes with equity must come with clean hands.”

In response, MyJoyOnline reported that Mr Boakye denied seeking any unlawful favours to retain his document. He stated that he surrendered his service passport under the presidential directive and only requested an official letter to transfer existing visas to his ordinary passport. He called the Majority claims a “red herring” designed to cover up financial questions.

Mr Boakye also alleged that the controversy was created to distract from parliamentary inquiries into GH¢16.4 million in evacuation spending. While the Majority cited GH¢49.7 million in total evacuation costs, Mr Boakye called on Mr Ablakwa to account for alleged discrepancies in documents provided to Parliament.

Parliament has not set a date for further hearings on the Foreign Affairs Ministry expenditure.

What’s next

Parliament has not announced a specific date to examine the Foreign Ministry documents.
