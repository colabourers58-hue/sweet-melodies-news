GoldBod refines up to 90 per cent of gold in Ghana, CEO tells Parliament

The Ghana Gold Board has recovered GH¢38.7 million in debts while expanding local jobs through 24-hour refinery operations.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Ghana Gold Board chief executive Sammy Gyamfi told Parliament’s Public Accounts Committee on Tuesday, 13 October, that domestic facilities now refine up to 90 per cent of exported gold.

Why it matters

Local refining keeps processing fees and silver by-products in Ghana, creating round-the-clock jobs for young workers. The board’s foreign exchange generation also helps stabilize the cedi and secure funds for gold purchases across the country.

The details

Adom News reported that processing gold locally allows refineries to operate continuously 24 hours a day, generating employment for young Ghanaians. Mr Gyamfi explained that keeping refining fees inside the country enables Ghana to retain greater value from its mineral wealth while supplying silver by-products to local artisans.

Graphic Online reported that committee chairperson Abena Osei Asare questioned whether the board trades foreign currency without a central bank licence. Mr Gyamfi answered that the institution does not purchase or trade currency as a commodity, but sells forex earned through statutory gold operations to commercial banks and the central bank for cedis.

According to Graphic Online, Mr Gyamfi explained that GoldBod wrote to the Bank of Ghana on 23 September 2026 after receiving policy approval from the Minister of Finance. MyJoyOnline reported that the institution remains on track to meet or surpass its 2025 forex generation target of 10.8 billion United States dollars.

Regarding outstanding debts, MyJoyOnline reported that GoldBod retrieved GH¢38.7 million out of total receivables amounting to GH¢55.8 million, representing roughly 69 per cent. Mr Gyamfi said legal proceedings cover GH¢7.5 million of the balance, while demand notices and negotiations address the remaining GH¢9.6 million.

MyJoyOnline added that aggregator firm Red Sapphire owes unpaid assay fees from testing bought gold. GoldBod recovered GH¢13.53 million from the firm, leaving GH¢2.61 million subject to ongoing discussions with the Bank of Ghana over who carries the liability.

What’s next

Public Accounts Committee members have requested copies of GoldBod’s written correspondence with the Bank of Ghana to confirm its operational authorization. The board aims to recover its remaining GH¢17.1 million in receivables before the end of 2026.
