Attack on Riyadh international airport injures several and grounds flights

Saudi authorities suspended operations at King Khalid International Airport following a strike on Saturday afternoon.

An attack on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh wounded several people and halted flight operations on Saturday.

Why it matters

The flight suspensions create immediate travel disruptions for workers and foreign nationals travelling through the Saudi capital. Security concerns across regional air routes continue to grow following warnings issued to commercial airlines.

The details

The Saudi General Authority of Civil Aviation confirmed that officials suspended airport activity to assess physical damage and determine the total number of injuries. A report by Reuters cited a witness who saw over twelve ambulances heading toward the location. Meanwhile, a local medical source informed Agence France-Presse that hospital personnel received instructions to prepare for a critical emergency.

An eyewitness at gate 401 in the domestic terminal told Agence France-Presse that people fled after seeing wounded individuals at the scene. Aviation tracking service Flightradar showed that all incoming flights to Riyadh faced cancellations or diversions. Foreign carriers, including Kuwait Airways, grounded their scheduled services to the Saudi capital due to the safety situation.

The incident follows earlier strikes on Thursday that killed three Saudi citizens, including a pilot, according to Saudi aviation authorities. Al Jazeera reported that Yemeni Houthi fighters have not officially claimed responsibility for Saturday’s incident, though the group recently warned commercial airlines against flying over Saudi Arabia, excluding the holy cities of Mecca and Medina.

BBC World reported that regional fighting escalated after Houthi forces seized key areas along the Red Sea coast, including Mokha port. Pro-government troops in Yemen subsequently stated that they had taken control of the critical maritime passage at the southern entrance of the Red Sea.

Western embassies, including the United States mission, warned their citizens to stay away from the airport premises. Al Jazeera reported that United States President Donald Trump stated his administration would evaluate whether to join Saudi strikes against Houthi forces.

The Saudi Ministry of Foreign Affairs instructed citizens travelling into the kingdom to contact local embassies if flight disruptions affect their itineraries. Airport management urged all passengers to verify flight statuses directly with their respective airlines before departing for the airport.

What’s next

Saudi civil aviation officials are assessing structural damage while commercial airlines monitor security conditions before deciding when to resume flights to Riyadh.
