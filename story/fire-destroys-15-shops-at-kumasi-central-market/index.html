Fire destroys 15 shops at Kumasi Central Market

Investigators suspect a power restoration fault triggered the Friday night blaze near Aboabo Station in the Ashanti Region.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

A Friday night fire destroyed 15 shops near Aboabo Station within the Kumasi Central Market in the Ashanti Region.

Why it matters

The blaze threatens the livelihoods of market traders at a major commercial hub ahead of the Christmas shopping period. Affected shop owners now await structural safety clearances and state relief before rebuilding their businesses.

The details

According to MyJoyOnline, the Ghana National Fire Service is investigating whether a power restoration fault triggered the outbreak. Ashanti Regional Fire Commander ACFO I Peter Tetteh stated that eyewitnesses noticed flames shortly after electricity returned following a brief blackout.

MyJoyOnline reported that emergency responders deployed five vehicles, including four fire tenders and one water tanker, to contain the inferno. Firefighters received their first response truck from Asokore Mampong at 8:39 pm and successfully created containment lines to stop the fire from destroying other parts of the market.

Fire officials confirmed that water supplies were sufficient during the emergency, although heavy market congestion hampered movement. Yen.com.gh reported that traders scrambled to save their goods, while local residents and shop owners trained in basic fire safety tried unsuccessfully to douse the early flames using portable extinguishers.

Reporting by 3News noted that Kumasi recorded two market blazes within three hours, following an earlier fire at a mattress factory in Sokoban-Ampayoo. Emergency services have not found any connection between the two incidents.

MyJoyOnline reported that Local Government Minister Mahama Ayariga visited the scene alongside Dr Frank Amoakohene, the Ashanti Regional Minister, and Kumasi Mayor Richard Ofori Agyemang Boadi. Mr Ayariga stated that presidential directives will guide the National Disaster Management Organisation in assessing damage to determine state relief for affected shop owners.

Government officials agreed to permit traders to temporarily rebuild their stalls ahead of the Christmas trading season once safety tests are completed. Structural engineers from the Kumasi Metropolitan Assembly will evaluate the damaged buildings, while ministers announced that stalled construction on the Kejetia Market Phase Two project will resume shortly.

What’s next

Kumasi Metropolitan Assembly engineers will perform urgent structural integrity tests on affected areas before traders can temporarily reoccupy their stalls. The National Disaster Management Organisation will complete its damage assessment to help government determine support measures.
