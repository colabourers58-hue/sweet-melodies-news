ECOM Ghana launches school farming clubs in Adansi North

The initiative aims to recruit young pupils into agribusiness across 40 basic schools to address Ghana’s rising average farmer age.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Sustainable Management Services under ECOM Ghana has introduced student agricultural clubs across 40 basic schools in 12 districts, Adom News reported.

Why it matters

The initiative provides practical agribusiness skills to basic school students while seeking to secure Ghana’s future food supply as the average farmer age rises to 55 years. It also encourages schools to end the practice of using agricultural labour as punishment for pupils.

The details

The official launch took place in Old Edubiase within Adansi North District, Adom News reported. Each participating school has secured farmland and started crop cultivation alongside livestock rearing to expose pupils to agribusiness, science, technology, processing, marketing, and entrepreneurship.

Emmanuel Baffoe-Bonnie, who acts as general manager for Sustainable Management Services at ECOM Ghana, said the programme responds to national concerns over an ageing agricultural workforce. Quoting Ministry of Food and Agriculture figures, he noted that the average Ghanaian farmer is 55 years old, while cocoa farmers are predominantly above 50.

Although agriculture accounts for over 20 per cent of Ghana’s Gross Domestic Product and employs more than half the workforce, young people often view farming as unattractive, Mr Baffoe-Bonnie stated. He explained that the new clubs aim to teach pupils practical skills in environmental conservation, climate awareness, nutrition, savings, and leadership.

Addressing child welfare, Mr Baffoe-Bonnie stated that child protection remains central to the initiative, distinguishing safe educational involvement from child labour. Club members receive guidance on personal safety and children’s rights, while assigned teacher coordinators undergo management and child safety training to serve as mentors.

To sustain the initiative, ECOM Ghana called for partnerships with the Ghana Education Service, Ministry of Food and Agriculture, traditional leaders, district assemblies, teachers, and parents.

At the launch event, the local education director for Adansi North, Alhaji Ibrahim Abubakar Amankwah, urged school teachers to stop assigning farm chores as disciplinary penalties. He stated that forcing students to weed or clear land for breaking rules deters them from seeing farming as an attractive and valuable activity.

Alhaji Amankwah urged educators to integrate textbook theories with practical experience on school farms to build student confidence and self-esteem. He added that moving agriculture out of textbooks into practical engagements will strengthen pupils’ belief in their own farming abilities.

What’s next

Participating schools will continue running farm projects alongside classroom teaching, while teacher coordinators receive ongoing mentorship and child protection training.
