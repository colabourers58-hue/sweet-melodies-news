Ghanaian bank assets reach GH¢500.2 billion as all 23 banks meet capital rules

Central bank governor announces total assets rose 20 per cent while non-performing loans dropped across the financial sector.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Ghana’s commercial banking industry expanded its combined asset balance by 20.47 per cent to reach GH¢500.20 billion through August 2026, Bank of Ghana Governor Dr Johnson Pandit Asiama announced.

Why it matters

All twenty-three commercial banks in Ghana are now compliant with legal capital rules after recovering from debt restructuring losses. This financial recovery protects household savings and provides a stronger base for everyday banking services across the country.

The details

The overall asset valuation grew from GH¢415.20 billion recorded during the previous year, MyJoyOnline reported. Addressing a Ghana Association of Banks gathering in Greater Accra on Thursday, 8 October, Dr Asiama confirmed that every one of the 23 operating commercial banks now satisfies mandatory regulatory capital rules.

Following the Domestic Debt Exchange Programme, 13 financial institutions had breached minimum capital standards because of asset impairments, according to 3News. Joint recovery efforts among commercial lenders, shareholders, investors, the central bank, and government helped restore complete compliance across the entire sector.

Financial stability indicators showed notable progress over the twelve-month reporting period. MyJoyOnline reported that the Capital Adequacy Ratio rose to 19.10 per cent in August 2026 from 18.28 per cent recorded twelve months prior, remaining comfortably above the 13 per cent legal baseline.

Loan portfolio quality across commercial lenders also reflected positive changes. According to MyJoyOnline, non-performing loans dropped to 15.66 per cent in August 2026 compared with 20.77 per cent in August 2025, though regulators require a further reduction to 10 per cent by December 2026.

Dr Asiama cautioned that simple capital compliance cannot guarantee long-term operational stability. 3News reported that the Bank of Ghana is preparing updated directives regarding credit risk management and liquidity coverage, while increasing supervisory attention on operational resilience, data security, and digital fraud.

The central bank conducted a structural review of bank business sustainability during 2025, sharing discovered vulnerabilities with institutional boards, MyJoyOnline noted. Regulators plan to execute a second round of business model evaluations in 2027 to ensure institutions manage risk effectively.

What’s next

Commercial banks are required to lower non-performing loans to 10 per cent by December 2026, with the central bank conducting its next business model analysis in 2027.
