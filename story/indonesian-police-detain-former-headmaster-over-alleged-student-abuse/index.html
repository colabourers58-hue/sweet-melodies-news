Indonesian police detain former headmaster over alleged student abuse

Ahmad Imanuddin Sumar faces investigation after former students uncovered evidence of assault at a Lombok boarding school.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Indonesian police have detained former boarding school headmaster Ahmad Imanuddin Sumar following allegations of sexual abuse, MyJoyOnline reported on Saturday.

Why it matters

The arrest highlights how former students gathered evidence to reopen a dismissed case after facing pressure to remain silent. It also demonstrates the impact of investigative broadcasting in bringing hidden abuse allegations to light.

The details

Mr Sumar, known to students as Abah, previously managed the Abul Barokat Islamic boarding school situated on Lombok island, according to MyJoyOnline. Police reopened the inquiry after a media report detailed allegations made by former students who had gathered evidence on their own.

MyJoyOnline reported that former pupils Ziadatur Rahmah and Sukma Rohana Fajar led the effort to document the alleged mistreatment. Mrs Rahmah stated that the former headmaster sexually assaulted her when she was seventeen years old, while Miss Fajar alleged that he attempted to rape her at the school.

Former classmate Yusron Azzahidi compiled a seven-page document outlining the claims and handed it to Nahdlatul Wathan Diniyah Islamiyah, the body managing the school, MyJoyOnline reported. The former students said that rather than offering support, the organisation pressured them to abandon their complaints.

During secretly recorded calls, Mr Sumar pleaded with the former students to safeguard his reputation, according to MyJoyOnline. Miss Fajar also recorded a conversation with Mr Sumar’s wife, who indicated that a statement Miss Fajar was forced to sign was intended to protect the school’s standing.

Following the publication of the report, twelve more former students submitted testimony to police, MyJoyOnline reported. Distressed parents also visited the institution to remove their children, with one mother telling reporters she wanted her daughter out immediately.

In a news conference reported by MyJoyOnline, Mr Sumar denied all claims against him. While he accepted that his voice was on the recordings, he stated they were not admissions of guilt.

The managing organisation denied attempting any cover-up and pledged to protect student rights during its internal inquiry, MyJoyOnline reported.

What’s next

Mr Sumar has resigned from his post and stated he will respect the legal process as police continue their investigation.
