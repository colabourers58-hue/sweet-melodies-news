Ghana repatriates 120 Nigerians rescued from cybercrime operations

Security agencies send home victims forced into online scams while preparing eight suspects for prosecution in Accra.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Ghanaian authorities have repatriated 120 Nigerian nationals who were trafficked into the country and forced to work in cybercrime operations, Communications Minister Samuel Nartey George announced.

Why it matters

The joint operation highlights how human trafficking and online fraud cross borders to affect West African security. For Ghanaian families and workers, dismantling these criminal networks protects local digital infrastructure and prevents local communities from becoming hubs for international crime.

The details

The rescue was conducted under Operation Cyberhunt, an intelligence-led sweep managed by the Cyber Security Authority in partnership with local intelligence agencies, Adom News reported on Tuesday, 13 October 2026. Security teams from the United Kingdom, Nigeria, and the United States also participated in the joint exercise.

According to MyJoyOnline, the 120 individuals had been brought into the country and forced into online criminal activities. Air force personnel and defence ministry officials assisted with their repatriation to Nigeria.

Security officers seized a significant collection of digital equipment from the suspects’ operational bases, Adom News reported. The items recovered include 136 mobile phones, 130 laptop computers, internet routers, personal jewellery, and 10 Nigerian passports.

Four motor vehicles were also seized by security personnel during the raids, MyJoyOnline reported. Officials revealed that investigators traced one of the confiscated vehicles to a theft reported in Canada.

In addition to those returned home, legal authorities are currently processing eight other individuals for prosecution, according to Adom News. Officials have not yet published the names of those facing court or detailed their exact charges.

The minister stated that the exercise formed part of government efforts to disrupt illegal networks and prevent Ghana from becoming an operational base for criminals.

“Ghana under President John Dramani Mahama will remain an unfavourable destination for cyber criminals,” Mr George stated, as quoted by MyJoyOnline.

Further investigations into the broader criminal network remain ongoing across multiple jurisdictions, according to reports from MyJoyOnline.

What’s next

State prosecutors are processing eight suspects for formal charges, while security agencies in Ghana, Nigeria, the UK, and the US continue multi-jurisdictional investigations into the remaining syndicate leaders.
