Donald Trump announces Russia Ukraine energy ceasefire deal

The United States chief eases Russian sanctions to cut global fuel prices while demanding new leadership in Ukraine.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

United States President Donald Trump announced an energy truce between Russia and Ukraine while lifting sanctions on Russian diesel to reduce global fuel costs.

Why it matters

Global fuel price spikes caused by Middle East tensions affect transportation and goods prices for families and workers worldwide. Reduced energy sanctions could alter international efforts to resolve the four-year war in Ukraine.

The details

Al Jazeera reported that Trump made a deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin to purchase 300,000 tonnes of diesel, with another 500,000 tonnes planned for November. The agreement aims to lower record global fuel prices caused by the conflict between the US, Israel, and Iran.

According to the BBC, Trump declared an immediate halt to attacks on energy infrastructure, but Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy initially said the agreement was “news to me”. Zelenskyy stated that Ukraine would accept a truce if Russia stopped its bombardment, but noted that Russian drones struck Ukrainian power facilities shortly after the announcement.

Trump publicly criticized Zelenskyy for targeting Russian oil refineries, suggesting that Ukraine should elect a new leader capable of reaching a peace deal. In response, Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha defended Zelenskyy, stating that the Ukrainian public holds the sole right to choose their president.

Zelenskyy said he would organize elections if Moscow agrees to a full 60-day ceasefire. However, he warned that lifting sanctions provides Russia with additional revenue to purchase weapons, fund military upgrades, and continue attacks on Ukrainian towns.

The diesel deal has faced opposition from several United States lawmakers. Al Jazeera reported that Republican Senator Thom Tillis questioned the legality of relaxing energy sanctions, arguing that the decision violates legislation passed by Congress prohibiting Russian energy imports.

Seven American senators sent a joint letter urging the administration to reverse the decision. International leaders, including Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and United Kingdom Prime Minister Andy Burnham, also reaffirmed their support for sanctions against Moscow.

What’s next

Lawmakers in Washington plan to challenge the legality of easing sanctions, while Ukrainian officials await formal details regarding the proposed energy ceasefire.
