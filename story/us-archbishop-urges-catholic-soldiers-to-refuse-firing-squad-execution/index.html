US Archbishop Urges Catholic Soldiers To Refuse Firing Squad Execution Duty

Archbishop Timothy Broglio says Catholic military members must ask to be excused from the planned December 3 execution of Nidal Hasan.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

United States Archbishop Timothy Broglio has instructed Catholic soldiers to request exclusion from a planned military execution by firing squad.

Why it matters

The stance reinforces Christian teachings on human dignity and the sanctity of life for believers globally. It highlights a direct moral conflict between government execution policy and religious conscience for military personnel.

The details

The directive follows an announcement by United States Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to execute and livestream the death sentence of former military major Nidal Hasan on 3 December. Al Jazeera reported that Hasan was convicted of killing 13 people during a 2009 shooting spree at Fort Hood, Texas, which left him wheelchair-bound after being shot by police officers.

In a statement released on Monday, Archbishop Broglio urged Catholic service members to seek exemption, stating that participating in the action contradicts church doctrine. He further questioned if the military should broadcast the execution of a person in a wheelchair, warning that doing so could harm the reputation of the armed forces abroad.

The Catholic Archbishop of Chicago, Cardinal Blase J Cupich, also criticised the broadcast proposal in a social media statement on Monday. He stated that capital punishment is unacceptable in all circumstances and questioned whether children should be exposed to a state-sponsored killing.

According to Al Jazeera, Pope Leo also posted a statement on Saturday condemning capital punishment. The Pope stated that human dignity is inviolable and argued that effective detention systems protect citizens while leaving room for redemption.

Defending the proposal, Defense Secretary Hegseth told media outlet Real America’s Voice that broadcasting the event provides deterrence by showing severe consequences for major crimes. He noted that the United States military has not carried out an execution by firing squad since 1945, when soldier Eddie Slovik was executed for desertion.

United States President Donald Trump approved the death sentence for Hasan after expanding allowed federal execution methods to include firing squads in April. However, President Trump told journalists on Saturday that he has not yet reached a final decision on whether the December execution should be livestreamed.

What’s next

The execution of Nidal Hasan is scheduled for 3 December. President Donald Trump is expected to make a final decision on whether to permit the livestream.
