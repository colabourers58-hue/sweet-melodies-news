Ato Forson promises New Economy will tackle unemployment

Finance Minister Dr Cassiel Ato Forson tells university students that new economic policies will aid graduates, farmers and small enterprises.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Finance Minister Dr Cassiel Ato Forson told university students that government will use the New Economy initiative to directly confront high national unemployment.

Why it matters

Graduate unemployment and high credit costs continue to place heavy financial strains on families and local business owners across Ghana. The proposed New Economy initiative aims to connect education directly to job opportunities while expanding market access for farmers.

The details

3News first carried the report on the address, which was subsequently published by Adom News, following the minister’s interaction with students and faculty at the Nyankpala Campus of the University for Development Studies.

According to the reports, Dr Forson emphasized that Ghana must no longer tolerate economic conditions where formal schooling fails to produce job opportunities, personal talent receives no backing, and productive labor delivers limited economic reward.

The minister stated that the New Economy strategy places employment at the central focus of national growth, drawing public attention to the individual human struggles behind headline unemployment figures.

“Unemployment is personal,” Dr Forson said, adding: “It is the graduate who has finished school but cannot find work. It is the young person with talent but no capital.”

In his presentation, Dr Forson highlighted the challenges affecting key sectors, pointing out that farmers routinely produce agricultural yields without predictable market access, while small business owners face high credit costs when attempting expansion.

For Ghanaian households, the minister observed that the financial and emotional strain is severe after relatives make sacrifices to fund higher learning. “It is the family that sacrifices to educate a child, only to watch that child sit at home,” he stated.

Rejecting the idea that widespread joblessness should be treated as permanent, Dr Forson remarked: “No serious nation can accept this as normal. The New Economy says Ghana must no longer accept it.”
