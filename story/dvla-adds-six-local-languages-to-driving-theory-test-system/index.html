DVLA adds six local languages to driving theory test system

Applicants across Ghana can now take their driving examination in Twi, Ga, Ewe, Dagbani, Nzema or Hausa.

WhatsAppX Facebook

Ghana’s Driver and Vehicle Licensing Authority has added six local languages to its computerized theory examination platform, Graphic Online reported on Monday, 12 October 2026.

Why it matters

This initiative opens up formal driver licensing for thousands of traders, commercial drivers and workers who communicate better in local languages than English. It helps ensure all drivers can demonstrate an understanding of safety rules regardless of literacy levels.

The details

The chief executive officer of the authority, Julius Neequaye Kotey, revealed the initiative during a Government Accountability Series media session at Jubilee House in Accra, according to Yen.com.gh.

Applicants sitting the mandatory driving theory test can now choose options in Twi, Ga, Ewe, Dagbani, Nzema or Hausa, Graphic Online reported. Built-in translation software permits test candidates to complete the examination in their preferred language.

Mr Kotey explained that the new measures target candidates who face difficulties reading or writing English, ensuring they can properly prove their knowledge of traffic regulations, road signs and safe driving habits.

According to the authority, work is currently under way to increase the number of Ghanaian dialects offered on the testing platform.

Beside the expanded language choices, the licensing body introduced an SMS notification system to alert licence holders about upcoming renewal and replacement dates, Yen.com.gh reported. The automated system delivers a reminder message one month prior to expiry and sends a second alert two weeks before the deadline.

Mr Kotey clarified that licence holders qualify for the text notifications provided the contact number used during their initial application has not changed.

Graphic Online reported that the authority is developing a similar text alert platform for vehicle roadworthiness renewals to prompt clients on due dates. Furthermore, electronic library facilities are being set up at branch offices across the nation so visiting clients can watch driver refresher and road safety videos.

What’s next

The licensing authority plans to integrate additional Ghanaian languages into the examination system and launch text message reminders for vehicle roadworthiness renewals.
