Russian strikes hit Ukrainian energy sites leaving 600,000 without power

The heavy missile and drone attacks hit Kyiv and other regions despite a recently announced energy ceasefire.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Russian missile and drone attacks hit energy facilities across Ukraine overnight, cutting electricity to 600,000 people and killing at least nine.

Why it matters

Widespread power cuts leave hundreds of thousands of civilian families in cold homes without basic electricity and heating during active conflict. The unexpected loss of early missile warnings also exposes vulnerable communities to sudden strikes without time to reach safety.

The details

The air strikes damaged two power plants supplying heat and electricity to Kyiv, alongside facilities in Kharkiv, Lutsk, Dnipropetrovsk and Zaporizhzhia. Ukraine’s largest private energy provider, DTEK, reported emergency power outages across multiple urban districts in the capital region, according to Al Jazeera.

Al Jazeera reported that three people died in Kyiv, three in the western city of Lutsk, two in Dnipropetrovsk and one in Zaporizhzhia. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko confirmed that 21 residents sustained injuries after a missile struck a residential building late on Tuesday.

The nationwide attacks occurred shortly after United States President Donald Trump announced that an energy ceasefire had been agreed between both countries. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine received no early warnings from Western partners about incoming ballistic launches, leaving residents without time to seek shelter, Reuters reported.

Ukrainian technology adviser Serhiy Beskrestnov said SpaceX restricted Starlink internet terminal speeds to 160 kilometres per hour overnight without prior notice. Defence ministry sources in Kyiv confirmed to the BBC that the speed limit disrupted frontline reconnaissance and missions designed to shoot down incoming Russian drones.

The Russian Defence Ministry stated that its forces targeted communications and energy infrastructure supporting the Ukrainian military, Interfax reported. Russian officials also stated that air defence units shot down 902 Ukrainian drones overnight, including 180 near Moscow and others targeting a Sberbank data centre in the Saratov region.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov stated that Ukraine did not deserve a respite, arguing that any ceasefire would allow Western nations to supply Kyiv with new weapons, according to the BBC.

What’s next

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky is scheduled to meet United Kingdom Prime Minister Andy Burnham in London on Wednesday to discuss accelerating air defence supplies.
