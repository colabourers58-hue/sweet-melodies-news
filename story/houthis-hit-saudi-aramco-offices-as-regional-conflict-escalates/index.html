Houthis hit Saudi Aramco offices as regional conflict escalates

A fire broke out at the oil firm’s Dhahran facility while flight suspensions expand and international leaders warn of global economic effects.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Yemen’s Houthi movement struck an administrative building belonging to Saudi Aramco in Dhahran, causing a fire on Tuesday.

Why it matters

The escalating strikes on energy sites and airports threaten global fuel supplies and disrupt international travel for workers and families worldwide. Financial leaders warn that the widening violence in the Middle East could trigger negative economic impacts across all regions.

The details

Al Jazeera reported that Saudi Arabia’s Ministry of Energy confirmed the attack in the eastern city of Dhahran. Energy officials told the Saudi Press Agency that emergency services contained the blaze and no casualties occurred at the state oil company’s headquarters.

The assault followed a warning from Houthi spokesperson Yahya Saree, who urged workers to leave Saudi oil facilities after the Saudi-led military coalition directed civilians to clear a fuel depot in Sanaa. Coalition air strikes subsequently struck an oil facility in Yemen’s capital, according to Al Jazeera.

Saudi forces intercepted two Houthi missiles directed at Riyadh, where falling debris wounded four citizens and damaged 15 residences, the civil defense authority told state media. Previous strikes on the capital’s international airport killed 13 individuals on Saturday and another victim on Sunday, leading major global carriers to cancel regional flights.

International reactions mounted as Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman stated that Houthi forces would encounter direct consequences for the strikes. United States President Donald Trump said the Houthis were “behaving very badly” and mentioned potentially requesting Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan to assist in protecting Saudi interests.

International Monetary Fund chief Kristalina Georgieva warned at a Bangkok conference that the escalating military actions could produce harmful economic consequences everywhere. Meanwhile, human rights monitors in Yemen said Houthi shelling in Taiz governorate killed five civilians, including a five-year-old child, over a twenty-four-hour period.

Reuters and Associated Press also verified that Houthi fighters hit the executive offices of the Saudi oil producer. Fighting between Yemeni government forces and Houthi combatants remains active in the southern al-Shaqab area, according to Yemeni defence officials.

What’s next

Saudi officials announced that Houthi forces will face consequences for the attacks, while clashes continue between Yemeni government troops and Houthi fighters in Taiz governorate.
