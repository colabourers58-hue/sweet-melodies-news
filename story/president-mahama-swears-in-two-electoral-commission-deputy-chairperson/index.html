President Mahama Swears In Two Electoral Commission Deputy Chairpersons

The appointments of Emelia Agbenyo and Dr Joseph Asamoah fill key vacancies at Ghana’s electoral body following recent leadership exits.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

President John Dramani Mahama has sworn in Emelia Afi Agbenyo and Dr Joseph Kwaku Asamoah as Deputy Chairpersons of the Electoral Commission in Accra on Monday, 12 October.

Why it matters

The Electoral Commission oversees national elections that determine governance and peace across all sixteen regions of Ghana. Trusted leadership at the commission helps ensure fair contests and maintains stability for workers, traders and families nationwide.

The details

The two appointments fill executive vacancies created after Dr Bossman Eric Asare resigned effective 31 July 2026 to return to academia and Samuel Tettey reached mandatory retirement age, 3News reported.

According to 3News, President Mahama instructed both appointees during their swearing-in ceremony to discharge their responsibilities without favouring any political candidate, political party or his own government.

Yen.com.gh reported that Dr Asamoah is a lawyer, chartered accountant and teaches law at Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology who previously worked as the commission’s Director of Finance. His professional profile notes that he designed an election results management system following an electronic transmission breakdown during the 2016 general elections.

3News reported that Ms Agbenyo serves as deputy director of operations at Ghana TVET Service and previously served as a governance sector spokesperson during the National Democratic Congress 2024 manifesto process.

Speaking on Joy FM, New Patriotic Party Communications Director Dennis Miracles Aboagye questioned her neutrality, alleging that her past political appointments and campaign statements threaten the independence required of electoral officers.

National Democratic Congress Deputy Director of Elections Rashid Tanko-Computer defended her appointment on Joy FM, stating that she is qualified and pointing out that past governments appointed individuals with political affiliations.

University of Ghana political science lecturer Dr Asah-Asante told TV3 that individuals appointed to oversee national elections must remain nonpartisan and above reproach to protect Ghana’s democratic stability.

What’s next

The new deputy chairpersons will officially assume their operational and administrative duties at the Electoral Commission headquarters in Accra.
