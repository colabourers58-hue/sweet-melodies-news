Kumasi market fires prompt emergency service call for new equipment

Firefighters save dozens of shops at Aboabo Market while fire officials warn that ageing vehicles threaten emergency responses in Kumasi.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

Firefighters saved 44 shops after a blaze destroyed eight businesses selling cookware and appliances at Aboabo Bola Market in Kumasi on Monday, 12 October.

Why it matters

The recent wave of market blazes threatens the livelihoods of hundreds of traders and small business owners across the Ashanti Region. Without working fire engines, emergency responders warn that future fires could devastate major commercial hubs before crews arrive.

The details

Adom News reported that no casualties occurred during the incident near Zuria FM, which affected eight out of 52 shops in the trading enclave.

Emergency personnel from the Asokore Mampong, Komfo Anokye Teaching Hospital, Bremang, and KNUST fire stations responded to the emergency with support from a regional headquarters water tanker, according to the Ghanaian Times.

The Aboabo fire follows three separate market outbreaks recorded across Kumasi over the weekend, 3News reported. Fires broke out at Sokoban Ampayoo, Kumasi Central Market, and the Tafo Nhyiaeso Moro Market between Friday, 9 October and Sunday, 11 October.

Speaking on Akoma FM, Ashanti Regional Public Relations Officer for the Ghana National Fire Service, DOIII Arhin Atta Mensah, revealed that the service has not received new fire engines for over 16 years.

According to 3News, the officer warned that while the Ashanti Region possesses 22 fire tenders, many are obsolete and breakdown constantly during emergency operations because spare parts cannot be found in Ghana.

DOIII Atta Mensah warned that without immediate government investment to replace ageing vehicles, emergency responders risk a potential shutdown of fire operations in certain areas of the region.

3News reported that affected traders across the affected Kumasi markets are appealing to the government for financial support to recover their losses, while fire officers have advised shop owners to maintain firefighting tools in their shops.

What’s next

Investigations into the cause of the Aboabo Bola Market fire remain ongoing, while fire service officials continue to press the government for urgent investment in new firefighting vehicles.
