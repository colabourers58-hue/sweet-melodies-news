US charges Saudi OpenAI engineer over hidden office recording device

Federal authorities in the United States say an engineer used AI tools to research and hide a bug in an executive conference room.

Sweet Melodies News
WhatsAppX Facebook

United States federal authorities have charged a Saudi engineer with planting a secret recording device inside an executive conference room at artificial intelligence firm OpenAI.

Why it matters

The case highlights growing corporate espionage concerns surrounding global artificial intelligence developers. It also shows how technology companies are deploying their own systems to detect internal security risks.

The details

According to BBC World, Abdulrahman Alfozan was charged in a California federal court with using an electronic device to secretly intercept oral communications inside his employer’s office.

Prosecutors said Alfozan had worked as an engineer at the ChatGPT creator since July 2025, as reported by BBC World.

BBC World reported that OpenAI staff discovered an encrypted listening bug wrapped in toilet paper inside a television USB port in San Francisco on 30 September, two days after it was installed.

The technology firm stated that it used its own artificial intelligence models to examine the device and identify the responsible worker, before firing Alfozan and alerting the Federal Bureau of Investigation.

Federal Bureau of Investigation Director Kash Patel stated in an official complaint that Alfozan allegedly placed the hidden recorder to secretly capture sensitive communications about artificial intelligence products.

Prosecutors alleged that Alfozan used a chatbot to search for instructions on building audio recorders, testing equipment, customizing Wi-Fi connectivity, and encrypting storage cards.

Security camera footage linked Alfozan to the room on 5 October when he returned to inspect the bug, before security officers discovered his wallet and a second recorder inside an unattended bag.

OpenAI confirmed that source code, model weights, and customer data were not compromised, while Alfozan appeared in court on Tuesday, facing up to five years in prison and a fine if convicted.

What’s next

Abdulrahman Alfozan appeared in court on Tuesday and faces up to five years in prison if convicted.
