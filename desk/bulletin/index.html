The bulletin

Today’s top stories, written to be read aloud on 94.3 FM. About a minute each.

  1. GRA Clarifies Digital VAT System Will Not Add New Tax

    The Ghana Revenue Authority says its new digital tax collection system will not add a fresh twenty per cent charge to your online subscriptions. Commissioner General Anthony Kwasi Sarpong explained on radio that ninety-two major foreign services, including Netflix and Spotify, already collect the required tax. That means prices for those subscriptions will remain unchanged for Ghanaian consumers. However, tax authorities have identified about one hundred other foreign digital platforms that currently do not charge Value Added Tax. Those services will gradually be brought into the system, which could increase total subscription costs for their users. The collection mechanism covers payments made through bank cards, mobile money accounts, and digital wallets. Physical goods bought online and imported into Ghana are not affected, as customs officers continue to tax them at the borders.

  2. Fire at Mallam GRIDCo substation disrupts power in parts of Accra

    An early morning fire at the Ghana Grid Company substation in Mallam has disrupted power supply across parts of Accra in the Greater Accra Region. The fire broke out at around two thirty-six in the morning on Wednesday, October fourteen, after a technical fault ignited a major transformer. Fire crews from Amasaman and Adjen Kotoku responded quickly to extinguish the flames and prevent further damage to nearby equipment. Communities affected by the temporary outage include Lapaz, Awoshie, Anyaa and Mallam. Officials confirmed that power has already been restored to several areas, while utility engineers work to reconnect the remaining neighborhoods and replace the damaged transformer. The company has apologized to affected customers for the inconvenience.

  3. Nana Fitz urges GFA and Sports Ministry not to rush Black Stars appointment

    Sports administrator Nana Fitz has urged the Ghana Football Association and the Ministry of Youth and Sports to avoid rushing the appointment of a new Black Stars head coach. Speaking on the national team’s technical leadership, Nana Fitz stated that authorities must take their time to select the right person for the job. The advice comes as football authorities prepare to make a decision on the technical direction of the senior national team. Supporters across Ghana, including workers, students and families, continue to follow developments surrounding the national team closely. News outlets including Adomonline, Modern Ghana and GhanaSoccernet reported his call for patience in the recruitment process. Football administrators are expected to evaluate their options carefully before announcing the next head coach for the Black Stars.

  4. Donald Trump announces plans for resource exploration in Greenland

    United States President Donald Trump says extensive searching for oil, gas and minerals is about to begin in Greenland. Writing on social media on Wednesday, the American leader claimed that the Arctic territory holds vast reserves of easily available fuel. The announcement follows a security pact reached last month between the United States, Denmark and Greenland. That agreement expanded the American military footprint on the island, allowing two new military bases and a missile defence system while confirming Danish authority. President Trump stated that Greenland offers vital protection against future aerial attacks aimed at America. Officials in Denmark and Greenland have not yet commented on the announcement.

  5. Ghana Premier League title race opens up after decline of top clubs

    The Ghana Premier League title race has opened up following a drop in form by Accra Hearts of Oak and Asante Kotoko. Football commentator Dennis Nyaku says the decline of the two national football powerhouses has allowed other teams to compete for the championship. Sports World Ghana reported his comments on the changing competition across the league. Meanwhile, Pan Africa Football reported analyst Asibey as stating that Hearts of Oak needed a victory to satisfy their supporters. For football fans, traders, and families across the country, the competitive season brings renewed excitement to local match venues. We will bring you further updates on the Ghana Premier League as matches continue.

  6. GoldBod refines up to 90 per cent of gold in Ghana, CEO tells Parliament

    Up to ninety per cent of gold exported by the Ghana Gold Board is now processed locally before leaving the country. Chief Executive Officer Sammy Gyamfi told Parliament’s Public Accounts Committee today that round-the-clock refinery operations are creating steady jobs for young Ghanaians. He also revealed that the board has retrieved about thirty-eight point seven million cedis from a total debt of fifty-five point eight million cedis owed by commercial partners. The remaining seventeen point one million cedis is being pursued through court action and negotiations. Mr Gyamfi clarified that the board does not trade in foreign currencies, but sells foreign exchange earned from gold sales directly to the central bank and commercial banks to fund gold purchases.
