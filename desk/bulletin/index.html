The bulletin

Today’s top stories, written to be read aloud on 94.3 FM. About a minute each.

  1. Mahama urges churches to protect vulnerable people at Accra congress

    President John Dramani Mahama has called on church leaders from across the world to protect children and turn Christian faith into practical community service. Speaking at the opening of the World Assemblies of God Fellowship Congress in Accra, President Mahama said spiritual leadership comes with a responsibility to safeguard vulnerable people. He urged churches to mentor young people, support local schools, and strengthen health ministries. The three-day event at the Accra International Conference Centre has brought together about ten thousand delegates from about one hundred countries. It is the first time the global meeting is being held in Africa. Church leaders prayed for the President and presented him with a leadership award during the opening ceremony.

  2. Hearts of Oak Prepare for Vision FC Following Port City Win

    Hearts of Oak head coach Nebojsa Kapor says his team must prepare better as they gear up to face Vision FC in the Ghana Premier League. The Phobians recently returned to winning ways with a narrow victory over Port City, a match that marked the debut of Prince Amevor. Kapor insisted that an earlier three-game winless run did not hurt team confidence, but warned that lessons must be learned from a past defeat in Tema. He has appealed to Hearts of Oak supporters to show up in large numbers for the match. On the other side, Vision FC enter the game on a five-match winless run. Despite that record, Wilson Asare Burton declared that Vision FC must win the upcoming match against Hearts of Oak.

  3. Brazil Senate Approves Shorter Workweek Ahead of Runoff Election

    Brazil’s Senate has approved a shorter workweek, giving President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva a major legislative victory ahead of the presidential runoff election. The decision by lawmakers comes at a crucial period in the South American nation as campaign activities intensify. Meanwhile, new polling data shows a close contest for the presidency. According to a Quaest poll reported by Reuters, candidate Flavio Bolsonaro holds a slight lead over President Lula in the runoff race. The approval of the shorter workweek is reported by Associated Press News as a major win for President Lula as voters prepare to head back to the polls to decide who will lead Brazil.

  4. Assemblies of God loans 10,000-seater dome for Ghana at 70 celebrations

    The Assemblies of God Ghana will loan a newly arrived ten thousand seat dome structure to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs for Ghana at seventy national celebrations. The church’s General Superintendent, Reverend Doctor Stephen Yenusom Wengnam, announced the gesture during the opening of the tenth World Assemblies of God Congress in Accra. He explained that shipping delays from Dubai prevented the church from setting up the facility in time for its own global conference. The dome has arrived at the port, but technicians need about ten days to erect it. Consequently, church leaders decided to move the structure to their Centenary City site and offer it to the ministry. Meanwhile, congress revival meetings will run every evening at five o’clock at the Accra Sports Stadium until Friday, sixteen October.

  5. Black Queens Name Lineup For Olympic Qualifier Against Mauritius

    Black Queens head coach Kim Lars Bjorkegren has named a strong starting lineup for Ghana’s twenty twenty-eight Olympic Games qualifier against Mauritius. Adomonline, Modern Ghana, and GhanaSoccernet report that the Swedish coach urged the national team to improve their finishing and stay clinical in front of goal. The Ghana Football Association and Graphic Online confirmed that a strong starting eleven was selected for the fixture. Africa Top Sports reported that the match takes place at Essipong in the Western Region as Mauritius travel for the second leg tie. Sports fans across the country and in the diaspora can follow live coverage of the team as they target progress toward the LA twenty twenty-eight Olympic Games.

  6. Archaeologists find world’s oldest shipwreck off Turkish coast

    Archaeologists in Turkey have discovered what is believed to be the world’s oldest shipwreck off the coast of Kekova Island. The trade vessel is estimated to be about four thousand seven hundred years old, placing it in the Early Bronze Age. Although the ship’s wooden structure did not survive, divers recovered more than thirty ceramic storage jars from about thirty metres under water. Laboratory analysis shows that the containers originated in the Jordan Valley and were likely used to carry olive oil, wine, or tree resins across the Mediterranean Sea. Researchers also found one jar with special markings that may have been used to churn butter or cheese. Turkish Culture Minister Mehmet Nuri Ersoy says the ancient artefacts will be displayed at a new underwater archaeology museum in Antalya when it opens soon.
